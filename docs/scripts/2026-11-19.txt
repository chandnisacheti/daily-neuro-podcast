Nora: A comprehensive systematic review just found that EEG neurofeedback training shows no robust clinical benefit for adults with ADHD over placebo control conditions.
Theo: The review synthesized data from seven studies, including three randomized controlled trials and four non-randomized studies, covering three hundred ninety adult participants.
Nora: They looked at a wide range of protocols, including theta-to-beta ratio training, slow cortical potentials, and QEEG-guided individual alpha peak frequency setups.
Theo: Here is where the methodology gets spicy. The non-randomized, open-label studies reported massive symptom reductions. But in the blinded, randomized controlled trials with proper active or sham controls, the effect size dropped near zero, hovering between negative zero-point-one-five and zero-point-three-zero.
Nora: Wait, so the clinical improvements completely vanished once researchers added a proper fake neurofeedback control?
Theo: Exactly. Cognitive outcomes like attention improvements were seen equally in both the active neurofeedback groups and the sham control groups, proving those gains were driven by practice effects and participant expectation, not neural modulation.
Nora: Ugh, that is a tough pill to swallow for consumer neuro-headsets and private clinics selling EEG retraining for focus and ADHD.
Theo: It's the classic neurotech trap. If your study design lacks a blinded sham control, you are just measuring the therapeutic context and placebo effect, not specific neuroplasticity.
Nora: It is disappointing for patients looking for non-drug options, but science requires real rigor.
Theo: Without rigorous sham controls, consumer neuro-claims fall apart under clinical scrutiny.
